# Support assistant — what the beta is, and what it isn't

For the support team and whoever signs off the trial. One page, plainly.

---

## What it does

A panel appears beside a Gorgias ticket. An agent asks for a draft reply; the assistant reads
the ticket and the company's published policy for that customer's market, and writes one. The
agent edits it, or ignores it, and copies it into the composer themselves.

## The one thing to be sure of

**Nothing reaches a customer unless an agent puts it there.** The assistant cannot send a
reply, edit a ticket, change a tag, alter a status, or issue a refund. It writes text into a
side panel. Every message a customer receives was sent by a person who read it first.

## Six things to expect

**It will sometimes decline, and that is correct.** When the published policy doesn't cover a
question, the assistant says so rather than inventing an answer. A confident wrong answer costs
more than no answer. Declining is the feature working — please don't report it as a fault
unless the policy *does* cover the question and it declined anyway. That one is worth reporting.

**Drafts are always in English**, whatever language the customer wrote in. This is at your
request, so agents can check what they're about to send. A draft an agent cannot read is a
draft they cannot check.

**Accuracy is not guaranteed, and agents stay responsible.** Check anything specific before
sending — figures, dates, delivery estimates, order details, and anything that differs between
markets. The assistant is a first draft, not a second opinion.

**It knows your published policy, not your team's habits.** It answers from the policy pages,
not from how a particular case was handled last month. If your team routinely does something
the published policy doesn't describe, the assistant won't know about it.

**Every draft cites its sources.** The panel shows which policy sections a draft came from, so
a doubtful answer can be checked against the actual page rather than argued about.

**It can be switched off in under two minutes**, by us or by you, with no software release. If
something looks wrong, say so and we'll turn it off first and diagnose after.

## Known limitations at launch

| | |
|---|---|
| Sign-in | One shared team token, not individual accounts. We cannot yet tell which agent asked for which draft |
| Conversation memory | Resets when the page is refreshed or the agent switches ticket. Refinements are lost |
| Attachments | Images and files are not read. A ticket whose meaning lives in a photo will be answered poorly |
| Past conversations | The assistant does **not** learn from your resolved tickets. That work exists but is not switched on, and will not be until a privacy review is signed off |
| Coverage | Published policy only. Internal procedures are used to decide what to say, never quoted |

## Reporting a bad draft

Send the ticket number and what was wrong with it. The useful detail is *what it should have
said* — that turns one complaint into a test case that stops the same mistake recurring.

There is no thumbs-up/thumbs-down button in the panel yet. It is planned, deliberately not
built for the beta, because a feedback button that nobody reads is worse than an email address
that someone does.

## What we would like from the trial

Whether the drafts are **worth editing** — a draft that takes longer to fix than to write from
scratch is a failure even if every fact in it is correct. That judgement is the point of the
beta, and it is not something we can measure from our side.
